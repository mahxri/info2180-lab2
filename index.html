Surviving My First Night in Minecraft

October 4, 2026 by Rahim McDaniel

My first night in Minecraft was far more stressful than I expected. I spawned in a sakura biome, punched a few trees for wood, and built a crafting table just as the sun started to dip below the horizon. By the time I had a wooden pickaxe, the sky was dark and the first zombie groans were already echoing across the hills.

I dug a small hole into the side of a hill, sealed the entrance with dirt, and waited it out with a handful of torches. It wasn't glamorous, but it worked. Lesson learned: always gather enough coal and wood before sunset, and never wander too far from your starting point until you have a bed and a few basic tools.

Building My First Redstone Door

October 7, 2026 by Rahim McDaniel

Redstone felt like magic until I realised it is really just electronics in disguise. After mining my first few pieces of redstone dust deep underground, I decided to build a hidden door for my base that opens with a pressure plate. It took several attempts, a lot of broken repeaters, and more than one accidental lava swim.

When the piston finally slid the wall open for the first time, I felt so accomplished. The design is simple, but it taught me how signals travel, how delays work, and how important it is to plan a circuit before placing a single block. Next up: an automatic wheat farm that harvests itself.
